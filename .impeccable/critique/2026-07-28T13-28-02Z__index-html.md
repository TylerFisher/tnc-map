## Design Specificity Verdict

**Low. Category-interchangeable with a brand skin applied.**

**LLM assessment.** Swap `data/members.json` for coffee roasters, EV chargers, or franchise locations and not one design decision would need to change — not the three filter pills, not the card list, not the popup, not the cluster discs. The only two strings on the entire surface that could not appear in a store locator are the sidebar blurbs: "Members defined by what they cover rather than where" and "Members whose home base is still being confirmed."

The brand inputs are supplied by the client, so applying them is compliance, not specificity. Where genuinely product-derived thinking exists it is engineering-side and invisible: the IQR outlier fence driven by one member in New Delhi, the `latin-ext` subset driven by one character in "Kaheāwai Media", the `beat` vs `unplaced` distinction. Every one of them is expressed on screen at 11–12.5px, or not at all.

The damning part: PRODUCT.md names *composition* as the defensible claim — Filipino diaspora, Philadelphia's Afghan community, Native Hawaiian readers, Alabama's Black Belt, a worker-owned tabletop-games newsroom. That material is in the data, it is short (longest description: 56 characters), and it is the one thing a competitor could not copy. The design puts it behind 69 individual popup clicks, most of which require opening a cluster first. **The only unforgeable asset is the one the interface hides.**

## Overall Impression

The engineering underneath this is honest and, in places, genuinely thoughtful. The design is not yet arguing for anything.

Everything on screen is built to help someone *find a newsroom*: three filter pills, a scrolling card list, per-member popups, clusters that collapse density into tidy discs. The recorded goal is the opposite — convince a program officer that this network is bigger and more varied than they assumed. Those two surfaces do not look alike, and this one was built before the goal existed, so the mismatch is a brief that arrived late rather than a failure of execution.

The single biggest opportunity: **the claim is in the data and it is short.** 103 one-line descriptions, none longer than 56 characters, naming Filipino diaspora newsrooms and Puerto Rican outlets and a worker-owned tabletop-games publication. Right now that material is reachable only by clicking 69 pins one at a time. Surface it and the map stops being a locator and starts being evidence.

And one thing must be fixed regardless of direction: **the layout breaks in the exact geometry it ships in.** Desktop is clean, which is precisely why it went unnoticed.

## What's Working

**1. The outlier fence and the honest "Show all N" control.** One member in New Delhi drove a real design decision instead of being papered over. The IQR fence in `src/bounds.ts` keeps the opening frame legible without hardcoding a US bounding box that would break on the next international member — and critically, `refreshOffFrameNotice()` tests against the *real viewport* rather than the fitted bounds, so the count is true rather than convenient. San Juan sits outside the statistical fence but is visible on screen, and the control correctly declines to claim otherwise. Honest defaults are rare.

**2. Triple-redundant tier encoding, entirely inside the brand palette.** `.pin--Community` carries an extra navy halo because brand gold disappears against pale Carto tiles — that is someone looking at the render, not the swatch. Alumni is simultaneously faded navy, a hollow pin, a hollow pill dot, and a text badge. PRODUCT.md's rule that tier is never carried by colour alone is satisfied four times over.

**3. Every figure in the summary comes from one filtered set.** Verified: filtering to Alumni yields "14 of 103 members · 9 on the map across 7 states and territories · 5 listed alongside" — mutually consistent. For a surface whose entire job is a numeric claim to funders, internal consistency of the numbers is the load-bearing property.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The layout breaks in the exact geometry it ships in

**What.** Verified independently at four viewports. The sidebar overflows past the footnote, so the footnote paints opaquely on top of the card list:

| Viewport | Sidebar bottom | Footnote top | Overlap | Page overflow |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 720×720 (real Ghost column) | 781 | 692 | **89px** | 61px |
| 390×844 (mobile) | 889 | 784 | **105px** | 45px |
| 360×640 (small mobile) | 797 | 580 | **217px** | 157px |
| 1440×900 (desktop) | 870 | 870 | none | 0 |

Desktop is clean. Every stacked layout is broken. The document also scrolls vertically *inside* a fixed-height iframe, producing two nested scroll regions in a Ghost post.

Separately and at **every** breakpoint including desktop: the 14 members with no `description` render as `Bottom Up MediaPublisher` — `.card__name` and `.badge` are both `display:inline-block` with nothing forcing a break.

**Why it matters.** 720×720 is the only geometry a funder will ever see. An artifact that looks broken makes its publisher look unreliable, and this one exists to argue for institutional credibility.

**Fix.** `.sidebar` inherits `min-height: auto` in the column flex context, which is what lets it overflow its track. Set `min-height: 0` and replace `max-height: 45vh` with `flex: 1 1 auto; overflow-y: auto`. Set `.card__name { display: block }`. Acceptance test: at 720×720, 390×844 and 360×640, assert `.footnote` top ≥ `.sidebar` bottom and `scrollHeight === clientHeight`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P0] Clustering renders the argument as its opposite

**What.** Verified mark counts against 69 mapped members:

| Viewport | Clusters | Loose pins | Total marks | vs. 69 members |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1440×900 | 15 | 11 | **26** | 38% |
| 720×720 | 15 | 11 | **26** | 38% |
| 390×844 | 11 | 3 | **14** | 20% |
| 360×640 | 6 | 4 | **10** | 14% |

`maxClusterRadius: 36` means a funder on a phone sees ten marks and is asked to infer a hundred newsrooms.

**Why it matters.** Product Principle 1 says "aggregate legibility beats individual browsability" — but clustering is not aggregate legibility, it is aggregate *erasure*. It is a locator optimisation for users hunting one result; here the user's job is to feel a quantity. This is the single choice most directly opposed to the recorded goal.

**Fix.** Drop `maxClusterRadius` to ~14–18 and set `disableClusteringAtZoom` at or below the initial fit zoom. Let dots overlap — overlapping dots read as *density*, which is the argument. Acceptance test: ≥45 distinct marks at 720px width.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable bolder`

### [P1] In production there is no headline at all, and the claim is set in chrome type

**What.** `.is-embedded .header__title { display: none }` removes the only `h1`. The largest rendered text at 720×720 is then **22px, and it is the Leaflet `+` button**. "103 members · 69 on the map across 29 states and territories" is 12.5px in `--muted`. A sidebar card name (13px) outranks the network summary.

Assessment B confirmed the accessibility half of the same defect: in the embedded document the heading outline **starts at `h2` with no `h1`**. The parent Ghost post has its own `h1`, but an iframe is a separate browsing context and does not inherit it, so assistive tech entering the frame lands on an orphaned `h2`.

**Why it matters.** The recorded success condition is "a funder coming away with a credible impression of reach." The sentence that delivers it is the smallest, faintest text on the page.

**Fix.** Split `#summary` into a figure line — Libre Baskerville, 26–32px, `--ink`: "103 newsrooms across 29 states and territories" — with the qualifier ("69 on the map · 34 listed alongside") retained beneath at 12.5px. Promote it to a visually-hidden-but-present `h1` in embed mode so the outline is valid without duplicating the host heading. Use only figures already in the data; PRODUCT.md's Evidence section forbids inventing others.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset`

### [P1] Accessibility floor: 36 undersized-text hits, a 2:1 control, and sub-44px targets

**What.** Three independent measurements converge here.

*Text size:* 34 tier badges at 10px and 2 group counts at 10.5px, against an 11px floor — 36 of the detector's 39 findings.

*Contrast:* the unpressed filter pill measures **1.99:1** analytically and **2.12:1** by sampling actual screenshot pixels. Both assessments derived ~2:1 independently. `src/styles.css` applies `opacity: 0.45` to the whole element, fading background and text together; a checker that ignores opacity compositing reports a comfortable 6.75:1 pass. Worse, the `prefers-contrast: more` block redefines `--muted` and `--line` but **never touches that opacity** — under high-contrast mode the pill still only reaches 2.27:1. The one control a low-vision user most needs is the one high-contrast mode fails to repair.

*Targets:* pills 27px tall, `Show all` 27px, zoom controls 30×30, sidebar links 17.5px tall, and map pins **15×15** — under a third of the 44px guideline in area, and the primary interaction of the entire product.

Everything else in the palette passes comfortably (5.57–15.86:1), and focus indicators are universally present and correct — 22 real Tab dispatches, every stop rendering a 2px navy outline.

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md records both a binding rule (tier never by colour alone — satisfied) and a network built around communities underserved by mainstream media. A 2:1 control undercuts that.

**Fix.** Raise badges to 11px and group counts to 11px. Replace the pill's `opacity: 0.45` with an explicit off-state — lighter border plus hollow dot, text held at `--muted` (already 5.5:1). Give pins a transparent hit area of ≥24px while keeping the 15px visual. Raise pills and `Show all` to 44px on coarse pointers.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P2] The artifact's closing sentence is an apology

**What.** At every breakpoint, the last thing on screen is `21 members are listed without a pin while their locations are confirmed.` The `.group__blurb` says the same thing ~500px earlier ("Members whose home base is still being confirmed").

Related: 57 of 69 mapped members have no keyboard or screen-reader path. Cluster markers are `role="button"`, `tabindex="0"`, with `title`, `alt` and `aria-label` all **null** — the computed accessible name is a bare number. A screen-reader user tabs "3… 2… 7… 5…" through 15 meaningless stops. Assessment B confirmed 22 Tab presses never reach the sidebar at all.

**Why it matters.** Peak-end rule: the closing sentence handed to a program officer is a data gap, not a network. And Product Principle 2 says no member disappears — for assistive-tech users, 57 currently do.

**Fix.** Reframe the footnote as capacity, not deficit: "21 more newsrooms — locations being confirmed." Nothing becomes less true. Drop the duplicate blurb. Give `iconCreateFunction` a derived `aria-label` composed from the child markers' existing city/state values (fabrication-free), and add a visually-hidden list of all mapped members after `#map` so the roster is complete for screen readers.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Minor Observations

- `FIT_PADDING: [36, 36]` is fixed pixels — 6% of a 1140px map, **20%** of a 360px map. That, plus a near-square aspect, is why 360px zooms out to a hemisphere.
- `worldCopyJump: true` produces duplicate "AUSTRALIA" labels in the "Show all" world view.
- Carto `light_all` returns multilingual labels — "AFRIKA / أفريقيا", "AMÉRICA DO SUL", "亚洲" appear in the world view. Unpolished for a US-audience artifact.
- 30 of the 34 sidebar badges read `PUBLISHER`. Near-zero information at high visual weight; a group-level badge would carry the same content.
- Sidebar descriptions are `--muted` 12px — the best copy in the product ("Community news rooted in Houston's historic wards") is styled as secondary metadata.
- `#summary` is `role="status"`, so every pill toggle re-announces the full sentence. Correct, but chatty under rapid toggling.
- Alumni's hollow pin and a generic "no data" hollow style are visually identical; on a light basemap a hollow ring reads as "empty" more than "past member".
- `Show all` reports 3 off-frame at 1440px but 5 in the embed harness — the default view loses more members as the viewport narrows, i.e. worst exactly where the funder is most likely to be.
- 404 on `/favicon.ico`.
- Font payload is 111.8KB across 6 woff2 files. The `latin-ext` half (51.9KB) is required by exactly one glyph; custom-subsetting would recover ~50KB without breaking the binding requirement.
- `nameNode()` degrading to a `<span>` for the 10 URL-less members is quietly correct.
- `scrollWheelZoom` off until click, re-disabled on `mouseleave`, honours Product Principle 5 properly.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` verified working (durations computed at `1e-05s`).
- The `#e4ded4` map loading background is a nice touch — brand-tinted rather than a grey flash — but there is no skeleton or loading text for a slow tile fetch.

## Questions to Consider

1. If you deleted the map entirely and set the 103 one-line descriptions as running editorial text, would a funder's impression of reach be weaker or stronger? If the honest answer is "stronger," that matters more than any bug in this report.
2. Name the funder question the tier filter answers. If you can't name one, why is it the most prominent control on the page?
3. Clustering exists so the map "reads at a glance." What exactly is being read — is 13 blobs a better number for TNC than 69 overlapping dots?
4. PRODUCT.md says composition is the defensible claim. It currently costs 69 clicks to see. Does the design believe its own positioning?
5. Alumni's status is recorded as explicitly undecided. Why does an undecided category own a permanent control that can remove 14 members from the evidence?
6. Product Principle 4 requires showing gaps honestly. Does it require *ending* on them?
7. Is there any zoom level at which "New Delhi + Wasilla + Honolulu + the contiguous US" is a good picture — or does that set need an inset rather than a zoom?
